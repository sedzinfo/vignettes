Greece’s Summer Economy: Who Comes, When, Where and What They Spend

What is this?

Greece is one of the most visited countries of Europe, and its tourism is squeezed into a few summer months and a few groups of islands. This story follows the tourists through the statistics of Eurostat:

  1. How many come?
  2. When do they come?
  3. Where do they go?
  4. Where do they come from?
  5. How much do they spend, against other holiday countries?
  6. How much does Greece depend on them?
  7. Where do they stay?

The data come from three places, all at Eurostat, the statistical office of the EU:

  • The statistics of tourist accommodation: the nights that guests spend in hotels, rented rooms and apartments, and campsites, each month since 1994, by region and by the country the guests live in. A night is one guest staying one night: a couple on a week’s holiday spend 14 nights. Since 2018 Eurostat also counts the nights booked through Airbnb, Booking, Expedia and Tripadvisor.
  • The survey of the trips of EU residents, which asks people in every EU country where they travelled and what they spent. It tells what a holiday in Greece costs its visitors, against a holiday in Spain, Italy or Croatia.
  • The balance of payments, which records what foreign visitors spend in each country, so it can be set against the size of its economy.

They are downloaded when the document is knitted, so the charts show the latest figures.

  x <- get_eurostat(id, filters = filters, time_format = "num", cache = FALSE)

156.3 million nights in 2025, more than ever.

In 2025, guests spent 156.3 million nights in the hotels, rented rooms and apartments, and campsites of Greece, 84% of them guests from abroad. Hotels alone had 119.0 million nights, 129% more than in 1994. In the first 8 months of 2026 there were 1.8% more nights than in the same months of 2025.

Until 2008 Greece counted only the nights in hotels and campsites; from 2009 it counts those in rented rooms, apartments and holiday homes too, so the bars jump in 2009. Since then the nights fell twice: in the debt crisis, to 80.6 million in 2012, and in 2020, when COVID closed the borders, by 73%. By 2023 there were more than in 2019.

5. How much do they spend, against other holiday countries?

Eurostat’s survey of trips asks people in every EU country where they went on holiday and what it cost them. The trips to a country include those of its own people on holiday at home, which are cheaper, as there is no long journey: so they are taken out here, and what remains are the visitors from the other EU countries. Türkiye, outside the EU, is in the comparison as Greece’s nearest competitor; all its visitors in the survey come from abroad.

Code 
# the value for the visitors to a country from the other EU countries: all EU residents, minus its
# own residents on holiday at home (Türkiye has none in the survey)
from_abroad <- function(x, country, year) {
  everyone <- x$values[x$geo == "EU27_2020" & x$c_dest == country & x$time == year]
  at_home <- if (country == "TR") 0 else x$values[x$geo == country & x$c_dest == "DOM" & x$time == year]
  if (length(everyone) != 1 || length(at_home) != 1) return(NA)
  everyone - at_home
}
spending <- function(year) {
  x <- data.frame(geo = destinations, name = eu_name[destinations])
  for (p in c("TOTXDUR", "TRA", "ACCOM")) x[[p]] <- sapply(destinations, function(d) from_abroad(spent[spent$expend == p, ], d, year))
  x$OTHER <- x$TOTXDUR - x$TRA - x$ACCOM
  x$trips <- sapply(destinations, function(d) from_abroad(trips, d, year))
  x$nights <- sapply(destinations, function(d) from_abroad(trip_nights, d, year))
  x$per_trip <- x$TOTXDUR / x$trips
  x$per_night <- x$per_trip * x$trips / x$nights
  x$length <- x$nights / x$trips
  x[!is.na(x$per_trip), ]
}

So, what does it mean?

Tourism is one of the pillars of the Greek economy, and one of its risks. Visitors from abroad spend 9.5% of GDP in Greece, and they spend it:

  1. In the summer: 72% of the nights fall from June to September. Greece has grown its summers, not its winters, so jobs and incomes in tourism remain seasonal.
  2. On the islands: 62% of the nights are spent in the South Aegean, Crete and the Ionian Islands, where there are 128 nights for each inhabitant of the South Aegean every year, with what that means for water, roads and housing in August.
  3. On long trips: a holiday in Greece costs its visitors from the rest of the EU €1,256, the third highest of the 11 holiday countries in the chart, and a night €145, the third highest. After inflation, each visitor spends about as much as before COVID: the growth comes from more visitors.
  4. Through the platforms: since 2019, nights booked through Airbnb and the like grew by 115%, the registered tourist accommodation by 9%.

2020 showed the risk: when the visitors could not come, 73% of the nights were lost in one year. Longer seasons, more regions and more visitors from outside Europe would spread it.

## What is this?

Greece is one of the most visited countries of Europe, and its tourism is squeezed into a few summer months and a few groups of islands. This story follows the tourists through the statistics of Eurostat:

1. **How many come?**
2. **When do they come?**
3. **Where do they go?**
4. **Where do they come from?**
5. **How much do they spend, against other holiday countries?**
6. **How much does Greece depend on them?**
7. **Where do they stay?**

The data come from three places, all at **Eurostat**, the statistical office of the EU:

- **The statistics of tourist accommodation**: the nights that guests spend in hotels, rented rooms and apartments, and campsites, each month since 1994, by region and by the country the guests live in. A *night* is one guest staying one night: a couple on a week's holiday spend 14 nights. Since 2018 Eurostat also counts the nights booked through Airbnb, Booking, Expedia and Tripadvisor.
- **The survey of the trips of EU residents**, which asks people in every EU country where they travelled and what they spent. It tells what a holiday in Greece costs its visitors, against a holiday in Spain, Italy or Croatia.
- **The balance of payments**, which records what foreign visitors spend in each country, so it can be set against the size of its economy.

They are downloaded when the document is knitted, so the charts show the latest figures.

from_abroad <- function(x, country, year) {
  everyone <- x$values[x$geo == "EU27_2020" & x$c_dest == country & x$time == year]
  at_home <- if (country == "TR") 0 else x$values[x$geo == country & x$c_dest == "DOM" & x$time == year]

spending <- function(year) {
  x <- data.frame(geo = destinations, name = eu_name[destinations])

## So, what does it mean?

**Tourism is one of the pillars of the Greek economy, and one of its risks.** Visitors from abroad spend `r percent(share_of("EL"), 1)` of GDP in Greece, and they spend it:

1. **In the summer**: `r percent(summer_share)` of the nights fall from June to September. Greece has grown its summers, not its winters, so jobs and incomes in tourism remain seasonal.
2. **On the islands**: `r percent(islands_share)` of the nights are spent in the South Aegean, Crete and the Ionian Islands, where there are `r round(in_region("EL42", "per_person"))` nights for each inhabitant of the South Aegean every year, with what that means for water, roads and housing in August.

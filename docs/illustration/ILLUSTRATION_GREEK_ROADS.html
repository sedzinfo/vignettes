Are Greek Roads Getting Safer? Road Deaths in Greece Since 1999

  • Show All Code
  • Hide All Code
  • View Source
Road deaths in Greece in Eurostat data since 1999: how far they fell, how Greece compares with the EU, why motorcyclists and young men die most, which regions are the most dangerous, and whether Greece can still halve its road deaths by 2030.

What is this?

Greek roads used to be among the most dangerous in Europe. This story asks how much safer they have become, with official data, in six questions:

  1. How many people die on Greek roads?
  2. How does Greece compare with the EU?
  3. Who dies?
  4. Why young men?
  5. Where are the roads most dangerous?
  6. Will Greece halve its road deaths by 2030?

The data come from Eurostat, the statistical office of the EU, which collects the road deaths that the police of each country record: a road death is a person killed on the spot or within 30 days of a road accident. They are downloaded when the page is rendered, so a new render brings in the latest year.

# a Eurostat dataset, without its missing values; the year as a number
eurostat_values<-function(id,filters) {
  x<-get_eurostat(id,filters=filters,time_format="num",cache=FALSE)
  result<-as.data.frame(x[!is.na(x$values),])
  return(result)
}

# people killed on the roads by age and sex, in number and per million inhabitants, for every
# country of the EU
killed<-eurostat_values("tran_sf_roadus",list(pers_cat="TOTAL",unit=c("NR","P_MHAB")))
total<-killed[killed$sex=="T"&killed$age=="TOTAL",]
deaths<-function(geo,year,unit="NR") {
  result<-total$values[total$geo==geo&total$time==year&total$unit==unit]
  return(result)
}
greece<-total[total$geo=="EL"&total$unit=="NR",]
greece<-greece[order(greece$time),]
first<-min(greece$time)
last<-max(greece$time)
eu_name<-c(setNames(eu_countries$name,eu_countries$code),EU27_2020="EU-27")
rates<-total[total$unit=="P_MHAB"&total$time==last&total$geo%in%names(eu_name),]
rates$name<-eu_name[rates$geo]
countries<-rates[rates$geo!="EU27_2020",]
countries<-countries[order(-countries$values),]
rank<-match("EL",countries$geo)
# the deaths per million people of an age group in the last year
age_rate<-function(geo,age) {
  result<-killed$values[killed$geo==geo&killed$sex=="T"&killed$age==age&
                          killed$unit=="P_MHAB"&killed$time==last]
  return(result)
}

# people killed by the vehicle they were in or on; the groups of the codes do not overlap, so they
# add up to the total. Those killed outside a vehicle, mostly pedestrians, are "OTH"
vehicles<-eurostat_values("tran_sf_roadve",list(geo=c("EL","EU27_2020"),unit="NR"))
groups<-list(`Car occupants`="CAR",
             `Motorcyclists and moped riders`=c("MOTO_2WH","MOTO_LE125","MOTO_GT125","MOTO_NSP",
                                                "MOP"),
             Cyclists=c("BIKE","EPAC"),
             `Lorries and vans`=c("VG_LE3P5","VG_GT3P5","VEH_GD"),
             `Pedestrians and others`=NA)
# the deaths of each group, by country and year: the last group takes what the others leave
by_group<-do.call(rbind,lapply(c("EL","EU27_2020"),function(geo) {
  x<-vehicles[vehicles$geo==geo,]
  do.call(rbind,lapply(sort(unique(x$time)),function(year) {
    y<-x[x$time==year,]
    all<-sum(y$values[y$vehicle=="TOTAL"])
    counts<-sapply(groups[-length(groups)],function(codes) sum(y$values[y$vehicle%in%codes]))
    data.frame(geo=geo,time=year,group=names(groups),
               deaths=c(counts,all-sum(counts)),total=all)
  }))
}))
recent<-by_group[by_group$time>last-5,]
share<-function(geo,group) {
  x<-recent[recent$geo==geo,]
  result<-100*sum(x$deaths[x$group==group])/sum(x$deaths)
  return(result)
}
motorcycles<-by_group[by_group$geo=="EL"&by_group$group=="Motorcyclists and moped riders",]
cars<-by_group[by_group$geo=="EL"&by_group$group=="Car occupants",]
riders_rose<-tail(motorcycles$deaths,1)>motorcycles$deaths[motorcycles$time==2019]

# the deaths of each region of Greece per million inhabitants, the average of the last years
regions<-eurostat_values("tran_r_acci",list(victim="KIL",unit="P_MHAB"))

region_last<-max(regions$time)

# the rate of Greece as a whole over the same years
greece_rate<-mean(total$values[total$geo=="EL"&total$unit=="P_MHAB"&
                                 total$time%in%unique(regions$time)])

1. How many people die on Greek roads?

69% fewer than in 1999. In 1999, 2,116 people died on the roads of Greece; in 2024, 664. Most of the fall came before 2015: part of it with the economic crisis, which took cars off the roads, and part with safer cars, better roads and more checks by the police. Since then the fall has slowed: 793 people died in 2015, 584 in 2020, when the pandemic emptied the roads, and 664 in 2024.

2. How does Greece compare with the EU?

Better than before, but still among the worst. Counted per million inhabitants, 197 people died on Greek roads in 1999, against 128 in the EU as a whole. In 2024 the figures were 64 and 44: Greece had the 3rd highest rate of the 27 countries, after Romania and Bulgaria.

3. Who dies?

More and more, motorcyclists. In 2020–2024, 38% of the people killed on Greek roads were riding a motorcycle or a moped, against 20% in the EU. Motorcycles are everywhere in Greece, for its mild weather, its narrow streets and its delivery riders; and a rider has no body of metal around them.

The motorcyclists are also why the fall slowed. Since 1999, the deaths of people in cars fell by 73%, those of motorcyclists and moped riders by only 49%; and since 2019 the riders killed went from 247 to 287. In 1999 they were 27% of all road deaths in Greece, in 2024 43%.

4. Why young men?

Everywhere, the young and men are the most at risk on the roads: the young lack experience, and both take more risks; men also drive more. 84% of the people killed on Greek roads in 2020–2024 were men. And counted per million people of each age, the young die most: in 2024, 122 people aged 18 to 24 died per million of their age in Greece, against 72 in the EU.

5. Where are the roads most dangerous?

On the islands and in the regions of long country roads; least in Attica. Per million inhabitants, the average of 2020–2023:

Code 
english<-c(EL30="Attica",EL41="North Aegean",EL42="South Aegean",EL43="Crete",
           EL51="Eastern Macedonia and Thrace",EL52="Central Macedonia",EL53="Western Macedonia",
           EL54="Epirus",EL61="Thessaly",EL62="Ionian Islands",EL63="Western Greece",
           EL64="Central Greece",EL65="Peloponnese")

average<-average[order(average$values),]
plot_ly(average,x=~values,y=~factor(name,levels=name),type="bar",orientation="h",
        marker=list(color=ifelse(average$values>greece_rate,greek_red,gray)),
        text=~round(values),textposition="outside",cliponaxis=FALSE,
        hovertemplate="%{y}: %{x:.0f} killed per million inhabitants<extra></extra>") %>%
  chart(paste0("Road deaths per million inhabitants, ",region_last-3,"–",region_last),
        "Eurostat, tran_r_acci. In red: above the rate of Greece as a whole.",
        margin=list(l=230,t=70,b=90),note_y=-0.1)

Attica, with its city streets and slower traffic, has the lowest rate: 45 per million. The highest is that of the South Aegean, 113. The islands count the deaths of their summer visitors, who drive and ride rented vehicles on roads they do not know, against their residents only, so their rates come out higher than the risk of their own people.

6. Will Greece halve its road deaths by 2030?

The EU has set itself the goal of halving the road deaths of 2019 by 2030, on the way to Vision Zero, close to no road deaths by 2050 (European Commission 2020). For Greece that means no more than 344 deaths in 2030, from 688 in 2019.

Code 
path<-data.frame(time=2019:2030,values=deaths("EL",2019)*(1-0.5*(0:11)/11))
recent_years<-greece[greece$time>=2010,]
plot_ly(recent_years,x=~time,y=~values,type="scatter",mode="lines+markers",name="Deaths",
        line=list(color=greek_red,width=2),marker=list(color=greek_red,size=7),
        hovertemplate="%{x}: %{y:,.0f} killed<extra></extra>") %>%
  add_trace(data=path,name="Path to half by 2030",line=list(color=dark_gray,width=2,dash="dash"),
            marker=list(color=dark_gray,size=5),
            hovertemplate="%{x}, on the path: %{y:,.0f}<extra></extra>") %>%
  chart("Greek road deaths and the goal of 2030","Eurostat, tran_sf_roadus; the EU's goal for 2030",
        "People killed",legend=legend_top,margin=list(t=100,b=90),y_axis=list(rangemode="tozero"))

On present trends, no. By 2024 Greece should have been down to 532 deaths to stay on the straight path; it had 664, 3% fewer than in 2019 instead of 23%. To reach the goal, the deaths would have to fall by 10% a year from now on. The fastest way there runs through the motorcyclists: they were 43% of the dead in 2024, and theirs are the deaths that rose since 2019.

## 6. Will Greece halve its road deaths by 2030?

The EU has set itself the goal of **halving the road deaths of 2019 by 2030**, on the way to *Vision Zero*, close to no road deaths by 2050 (European Commission 2020). For Greece that means no more than `r number(deaths("EL",2019)/2)` deaths in 2030, from `r number(deaths("EL",2019))` in 2019.

```{r target}
#| fig-alt: "Line chart of the road deaths of Greece since 2010 against the straight path to half of the 2019 deaths in 2030: the deaths stay well above the path"
path<-data.frame(time=2019:2030,values=deaths("EL",2019)*(1-0.5*(0:11)/11))
recent_years<-greece[greece$time>=2010,]
plot_ly(recent_years,x=~time,y=~values,type="scatter",mode="lines+markers",name="Deaths",
        line=list(color=greek_red,width=2),marker=list(color=greek_red,size=7),
        hovertemplate="%{x}: %{y:,.0f} killed<extra></extra>") %>%
  add_trace(data=path,name="Path to half by 2030",line=list(color=dark_gray,width=2,dash="dash"),
            marker=list(color=dark_gray,size=5),
            hovertemplate="%{x}, on the path: %{y:,.0f}<extra></extra>") %>%
  chart("Greek road deaths and the goal of 2030","Eurostat, tran_sf_roadus; the EU's goal for 2030",
        "People killed",legend=legend_top,margin=list(t=100,b=90),y_axis=list(rangemode="tozero"))
```

**On present trends, no.** By `r last` Greece should have been down to `r number(path$values[path$time==last])` deaths to stay on the straight path; it had `r number(deaths("EL",last))`, `r round(100*(1-deaths("EL",last)/deaths("EL",2019)))`% fewer than in 2019 instead of `r round(100*(1-path$values[path$time==last]/deaths("EL",2019)))`%. To reach the goal, the deaths would have to fall by `r round(100*(1-(0.5*deaths("EL",2019)/deaths("EL",last))^(1/(2030-last))))`% a year from now on. The fastest way there runs through the motorcyclists: they were `r round(100*tail(motorcycles$deaths,1)/tail(motorcycles$total,1))`% of the dead in `r last``r if(riders_rose) ", and theirs are the deaths that rose since 2019"`.
